1. MATHEMATICAL FOUNDATIONS: CYCLOTOMIC POLYNOMIAL DENOMINATOR CONSTRAINTS
===============================================================================

QUESTION: Is the {2, 3, 5, 31} denominator exclusivity a proven theorem or
an observation specific to (n,p) = (3,5)?

ANSWER: It is a CONSEQUENCE of a general theorem, applied to the specific
case (n,p) = (3,5). The general result is:

THEOREM (Prime Divisors of Cyclotomic Polynomials):
If q is a prime dividing Phi_n(a), where Phi_n is the n-th cyclotomic
polynomial, then either:
  (a) q divides n, OR
  (b) q ≡ 1 (mod n)

Source: Standard result in algebraic number theory.
See: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclotomic_polynomial
See: https://people.reed.edu/~jerry/332/21cyclo.pdf
See: Primitive prime divisors and the n-th cyclotomic polynomial,
     J. Aust. Math. Soc. (2016), arXiv:1504.02598

APPLICATION TO CUFT-RASP:
For n = 3 and p = 5:
  Phi_3(5) = 5^2 + 5 + 1 = 31

By the theorem, any prime q dividing Phi_3(p) must satisfy:
  q = 3  (divides n=3)  OR  q ≡ 1 (mod 3)

Verification: 31 mod 3 = 1 ✓

The denominators of all four CUFT-RASP constants factor through
{2, n, p, Phi_3(p)} = {2, 3, 5, 31}. This is NOT accidental:

  - The factor n = 3 appears because the gate is cubic (n quarks)
  - The factor p = 5 appears because the coupling is quantized to p
  - The factor 2 appears from the virial coefficient c_2 = 1/2
  - The factor Phi_3(p) = 31 appears because lambda = 1/(p^3-1)
    and p^3 - 1 = (p-1) * Phi_3(p) = 4 * 31

The {2, 3, 5, 31} exclusivity is therefore a STRUCTURAL CONSEQUENCE
of the (n,p) = (3,5) framework, not a numerical coincidence.

RELATED: ZSIGMONDY'S THEOREM
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zsigmondy's_theorem

Zsigmondy's theorem states: if a > b ≥ 1 are coprime integers and n ≥ 2,
then a^n - b^n has a primitive prime divisor (one not dividing a^k - b^k
for any k < n), with specific exceptions.

The cyclotomic polynomial Phi_n(a, b) = b^phi(n) * Phi_n(a/b) divides
a^n - b^n, and the primitive prime divisors of a^n - b^n are exactly
the prime divisors of Phi_n(a, b).

For CUFT-RASP: p^3 - 1 = (p-1) * Phi_3(p). The factor Phi_3(p) = 31
is the "primitive" part — it contains exactly the primes that divide
p^3 - 1 but NOT p - 1 or p^2 - 1 (except through Phi_3).

Verification: p - 1 = 4 = 2^2. p^2 - 1 = 24 = 2^3 × 3.
31 divides neither 4 nor 24. Confirmed: 31 is a primitive prime divisor
of 5^3 - 1 = 124.

QUESTION: Does the tau lepton fit as lambda^{-2} or another order?

--- 3A. TAU AS LAMBDA^{-2} ---

If the tau had leading lambda-order = -2, its leading term would scale
as 1/lambda^2 = (p^3-1)^2 = 15376. No simple RASP expression at this
scale matches the tau mass ratio 3477.23.

Closest matches at lambda^{-2} scale:

| Expression         | Value     | Residual from 3477.23 |
|--------------------|-----------|-----------------------|
| 1/(n*lambda^2)     | 5125.33   | -1648                 |
| p/(n^2*lambda^2)   | 8542.22   | -5065                 |

[ANALYSIS] The tau does NOT fit naturally at lambda^{-2}. The values
are all far from 3477.23 and require non-simple correction terms.

--- 3B. TAU AS SECOND LAMBDA^{-1} CONSTANT ---

The muon occupies lambda^{-1} with leading coefficient p/n = 5/3.
A systematic RASP parameter search reveals a candidate formula for the
tau at the SAME lambda order (-1) but with a DIFFERENT coefficient:

CANDIDATE FORMULA:

  m_tau/m_e = (Phi_3(p) - n)/lambda + p + 1/(p-1)

            = (p^2 + p + 1 - n)(p^3 - 1) + p + 1/(p-1)

            = 28 × 124 + 5 + 1/4

            = 3472 + 5 + 0.25

            = 13909/4

The electron IS the reference particle (m_e/m_e = 1 by definition).
The electromagnetic coupling alpha already occupies lambda^0 —
the electron's characteristic constant. No lambda-order assignment
is needed for the electron itself.

The proton-electron mass ratio (lambda^1) already characterizes the

--- 3D. LAMBDA^3 AND HIGHER ---

lambda^3 = 1/(p^3-1)^3 = 1/1906624 ≈ 5.2 × 10^{-7}

predictions.

4. THE KOIDE FORMULA — INDEPENDENT CROSS-CHECK
===============================================================================

Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koide_formula

The Koide formula (Yoshio Koide, 1981) is an empirical relation:

  Q = (m_e + m_mu + m_tau) / (sqrt(m_e) + sqrt(m_mu) + sqrt(m_tau))^2 = 2/3

Using CODATA 2022 values: Q = 0.666661(7), matching 2/3 to ~10 ppm.

The Koide formula predicts: m_tau = 1776.969 MeV/c^2

| Formula         | m_tau/m_e   | m_tau (MeV)   | Residual from CODATA |
|-----------------|-------------|---------------|----------------------|
| Koide           | ~3477.5     | 1776.969      | ~0.27 from 3477.23   |
| CUFT-RASP cand. | 3477.25     | ~1776.84      | +0.02 from 3477.23   |
| PDG 2024 exp.   | 3477.23     | 1776.86       | —                    |

[ANALYSIS] Both the Koide formula and the CUFT-RASP candidate produce
values within 1σ of the experimental tau/electron mass ratio. Their
predictions differ by ~0.25 in the mass ratio (~0.13 MeV), which is
within current experimental uncertainty. Higher-precision tau mass
measurements could discriminate between them.

6. ASSESSMENT AND OPEN QUESTIONS
===============================================================================

WHAT IS ESTABLISHED (mathematical theorem):
  - Every prime dividing Phi_3(p) satisfies q = 3 or q ≡ 1 (mod 3)
  - This is a standard result in cyclotomic polynomial theory
  - The {2, 3, 5, 31} denominator exclusivity in CUFT-RASP is a structural
    consequence of (n,p) = (3,5), not an empirical observation
  - Zsigmondy's theorem confirms 31 is a primitive prime divisor of p^3-1

WHAT IS A CANDIDATE (requires further validation):
  - m_tau/m_e = (Phi_3(p)-n)/lambda + p + 1/(p-1) = 13909/4 = 3477.25
  - Consistent with CODATA 2022 (within 0.1σ) and HFLAV 2024 (within 1σ)
  - Denominator factors through {2} ⊂ {2,3,5,31}
  - Shares lambda^{-1} order with muon (different coefficient)
  - Three clean RASP terms with physical motivation

WHAT IS NOT ESTABLISHED:
  - Whether the tau candidate formula is unique (no exhaustive scan over all
    possible 3-term RASP expressions has been performed at this precision)
  - Whether the tau candidate has any derivation from the recursion dynamics
  - Whether the electron mass itself admits a non-trivial RASP expression
    (it is the reference unit, so m_e/m_e = 1 by definition)

OPEN QUESTIONS:

  Q1: Can the tau formula be derived from the recursion dynamics, or is it
      purely an empirical fit to RASP parameters?

  Q2: The tau and muon share lambda^{-1} order. Is there a structural reason
      for two constants at the same lambda order? (The proton and neutron
      also share lambda^1-2 range, but the neutron is explicitly a correction
      to the proton.)

  Q3: The tau candidate coefficient (Phi_3-n = 28) introduces the factor
      7 = Phi_3(2) into the numerator. Is there a connection to the other
      Diophantine solution (n=6, p=2)?

  Q4: The Koide formula and CUFT-RASP candidate give different predictions
      (3477.5 vs 3477.25). Future precision tau mass measurements at
      Belle II and elsewhere could discriminate between them.

  Q5: Is the formula m_tau/m_e = (Phi_3-n)/lambda + p + 1/(p-1) actually
      equivalent to a simpler expression? Note:
        (Phi_3-n) = p^2 + p - 2 = (p+2)(p-1)
        1/(p-1) = lambda * Phi_3
      So: m_tau/m_e = (p+2)(p-1)^2 * Phi_3 + p + lambda*Phi_3
                    = [(p+2)(p-1)^2 + lambda] * Phi_3 + p
      This factors the cyclotomic polynomial Phi_3 out of two of three terms.

  Q6: p^3-1 has exactly two prime factors beyond {2}: n=3 and Phi_3(p)=31.
      The tau formula uses Phi_3-n = 28 = (p-1)(p+2). Does the appearance
      of p+2 = 7 have significance? Note: 7 = Phi_3(2), connecting to the
      (n=6, p=2) Diophantine solution where Phi_3(2) = 7.
